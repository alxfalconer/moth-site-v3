Projects are where the
research meets an audience.

Each collaboration pairs a cultural partner with quantum computation, testing what the technology makes possible in music, games and visual art rather than arguing for it in the abstract.

Some run on real quantum hardware, others on classical simulations of quantum methods, and we label which is which. Together they sketch the mechanics of a quantum-native media.

Projects, Products & Collaborations. From immersive games and virtual worlds, to new audio formats and generative visual tools, we’re bridging the gap to the future today.

Gaming

Space Moths

A Roblox game built on our Quantum Reservoir Computing system and powered on-demand by a quantum processing unit (QPU).

The first demonstration of how quantum algorithms can be integrated directly into a live game experience to supply content for a massively multiplayer game. A collaboration with Onward Studios, made possible by VTT, IBM and IQM providing quantum hardware access.

Music

Ila — Recurse

A world’s first. Infinite-mix is a new sonic format – a continuously generating real-time stream of music.

Trained on music composed by artist ILĀ, RECURSE [infinite mix] represents an artist-first approach to generative AI creativity, contrasting autonomous music gen AI platforms on the market today.

Gaming

Quantum Backrooms

A Backrooms-inspired game challenging users to navigate labyrinths shaped by the behavior and architecture of an actual quantum processing unit (QPU).

The resulting layouts are a superposition of possible map states that enable players to experience quantum dynamics first-hand.

Visual arts

Roman Lipski & Quantum Brush

A collaboration with contemporary artist Roman Lipski to turn quantum physics into a painter’s tool running on real quantum hardware.

Each brush is built from a different quantum phenomenon, acting on color rather than composition, to produce imperfect, unpredictable strokes.

Music

Actias

A software synthesizer that turns the physics of quantum computers into a new way to compose.

Actias lets you patch qubits to instruments and collapse the possibilities into sound, giving every patch a controlled, never-quite-repeating unpredictability.
