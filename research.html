Media is shaped by the
technological substrate of its time.

From the printing press and photography to broadcast, digital and social media, each new technology gives rise to new mechanics for how content is created, distributed and experienced. Today’s systems, built on recommendation algorithms and generative AI, are straining under mass-disinformation, homogenised content and eroding trust. The next shift won’t come from refining them, but from a fundamentally new paradigm: quantum computing.

Quantum computing processes information in a radically different way — leveraging phenomena like superposition and entanglement rather than stepping through binary logic. That opens a unique set of affordances and a new kind of media mechanics, which we at MOTH are uncovering every day.

Superposition

A quantum system holds many states at once, each with its own weight, instead of settling on one value.

For a creative tool, that means keeping every option alive at once: a synth voicing several tunings together, a generator carrying a whole space of variations before you commit.

Actias ↗

Interference

Overlapping quantum possibilities add and cancel like waves, reinforcing some outcomes and erasing others. It’s how computation steers toward a result: not by choosing, but by tuning which patterns survive.

In sound and image it yields textures ordinary mixing can’t reach, the basis of our quantum-audio and QuantumBlur work.

quantum-audio ↗

Measurement

A quantum system holds many possibilities until measured; in that instant it commits to one and the rest fall away.

In a tool, it’s the moment a generative space becomes finished: the frame that renders, the level that spawns, the take that plays.

Quantum Backrooms ↗

Entanglement

Two quantum parts can be linked so that knowing one tells you about the other, however far apart. They behave as a single connected system.

Creatively, it’s a way to make elements move together: linked characters, correlated worlds, sound and image responding as one.

Quantum Randomness

Quantum measurements are fundamentally, provably random, not a formula imitating chance.

Fed into a game or generator, that yields content no algorithm could predict or repeat. Space Moths pulls exactly this from real quantum hardware, on demand.

Space Moths ↗

Uncertainty

You can’t pin down every property of a quantum system at once: sharpen one and another blurs.

Treated as a material rather than a limit, it becomes expressive: controlled ambiguity, a blur that carries information instead of noise. The idea behind QuantumBlur.

We’re not waiting for
fault tolerant computers.

The full power of quantum computing will be unlocked with fault-tolerance. We’re finding concrete ways that quantum advantages can empower creatives and the creative industries, looking at both near-term quantum utility and longterm quantum advantage. At the same time, we know that creatives are inspired by new perspectives.

Usefulness does not just come from computational power, but from providing new opportunities for expression and aesthetic innovation. This makes NISQ quantum hardware useful for creativity even before quantum advantages emerge. Through our research we find ever more sophisticated ways to bring near-term quantum hardware to games, music and visual media.
